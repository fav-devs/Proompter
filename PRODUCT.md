# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Proompter is used by speakers, creators, presenters, worship leaders, educators, founders, and solo operators who need a script to stay readable while they perform or record. They often move between desktop prep and phone-based rehearsal or live delivery. On mobile, they need quick access to the current script, large readable controls, and a stage that stays legible without precision tapping.

## Product Purpose
Proompter is a speech-aware teleprompter for writing, organizing, rehearsing, and delivering scripts with less friction. The product should help users keep eye-line, maintain pacing, and recover quickly when they pause, restart, or switch scripts. Success means the interface disappears into the task: the script stays readable, controls stay reachable, and follow mode feels trustworthy.

## Brand Personality
Calm, editorial, dependable. The product should feel warm and typographic, closer to a printed research journal than a glossy SaaS dashboard. Keep the existing palette direction, preserve the product's current identity, and borrow from Anthropic's compositional restraint rather than copying its interface literally.

## Anti-references
Do not turn Proompter into a generic dashboard, a neon creator tool, or a glassy gradient-heavy AI app. Avoid crowded mobile control clusters, decorative shadows, candy-colored states, pill-heavy badges, and visual noise that competes with the script itself. Do not discard the current color system in favor of a direct Anthropic clone.

## Design Principles
1. Reading comes first: stage legibility and script flow matter more than decorative UI.
2. Mobile is a real surface, not a scaled-down desktop: thumb reach, panel order, and editing flow must be intentional.
3. Warm restraint beats novelty: strong typography, quiet surfaces, and sparse accent usage should carry the product.
4. Controls should feel instrument-like: compact, precise, and dependable, with clear states and minimal ambiguity.
5. Follow mode and playback are trust features: supporting UI should reduce uncertainty, not add motion or clutter.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Prioritize readable type, strong contrast, large enough touch targets, and clear focus states. Support reduced-motion users by avoiding decorative motion and relying on stateful feedback instead. On mobile, assume one-handed use and avoid requiring fine motor precision for core actions like play, rewind, follow, and fullscreen.
